From words to watering

How it works

A simple path from an instruction to a robot action. The learned models are still catching up with the expert.

Instruction

One line says which plant to water and how much. It might ask for the left plant or just a little water.

Scripted expert

A hand-written controller reaches, grasps, pours and returns the can. It makes repeatable examples for a learner to copy.

Demonstrations

We save the expert’s actions and camera views as a LeRobot dataset. The cameras show the scene, eye and wrist views.

ACT policy

ACT learns robot movements from those examples. The best current version reaches 41/50 held-out scenes.

SmolVLA

Next, teach a vision-language-action model to follow the sentence directly. It learns from the instruction and camera images.

95% Wilson intervals. Success means at least 70% of the poured water lands in the target pot, the can ends upright, only the hand touches objects, and the episode finishes within its time limit.

Dev seeds are used to choose and tune a model; held-out seeds are saved for a final check and are not tuned on.

A useful surprise

More data made it worse.

Twice the demonstrations did not mean twice the skill. Robot learning likes to keep us humble.

v1 · 200 demonstrations

41/50held-out successes

The first ACT policy is still the best one so far.

v2 · 400 demonstrations

23/50held-out successes

More examples, lower held-out success. Ouch.

Why? Offline action error was similar: v2 could copy recorded demonstrations about as well. In a live run, small mistakes change what the robot sees next, then compound. That closed-loop drift is where v2 falls behind, especially at negative can angles.

What the numbers don’t show

Challenges, honestly.

Some are fixed. Some are accepted shortcuts. A few are still on the to-do list.

Fixed

The fake perfect score

The pots had no collision, so the can could pass through them. Solid pots and contact checks made the score honest.

Accepted

AV1 video glitches

A few v2 wrist-camera frames have a one-frame encoding glitch. The raw recorded images are clean.

Fixed

Hub uploads that hung

IPv6 packet drops stalled uploads on this connection. Resumable uploads and an IPv4-only workaround got them through.

Accepted

Friction-only grasp

The can is held by friction. The thumb curls last; no special latch or suction is involved.

Open

A narrow can layout

Layouts that bring the can or open hand into a pot are redrawn. Results do not yet cover the full ±8 cm range.

Accepted

Water with training wheels

Water moves, but does not push back. The can’s water weight stays fixed during a pour.

Accepted

A generous time limit

Episodes get 45 seconds, not the 30 seconds first planned. A full watering takes its time.

Accepted

The can hides the water

The solid can body blocks the camera’s view of the water level.

Accepted

Small pot contacts

Can-body contacts under 10 N are not counted; they are hard to tell apart from the fingers touching the can.
